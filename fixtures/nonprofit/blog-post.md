People ask me what a Saturday at Wrenfield Community Pantry is like. I usually say "busy." That's true, but it doesn't tell you much. So this time I'm going to walk you through one.

## 7:30 a.m. The garden comes first

Before the doors open, a few of us head out back to the garden. In July, that means tomatoes. Lots of them. We pick into old milk crates and carry them inside while the morning is still cool.

The garden started small. A few raised beds and a lot of hope. This past year we harvested 2,300 pounds of produce, according to our garden harvest log. I still can't believe that number. Every pound of it went straight to the Saturday table.

Ray, one of our longtime volunteers, likes to say the garden is the only part of the pantry that grows on its own. He's wrong. It grows because people like Ray show up at 7:30 with gloves on.

## 8:15 a.m. Setting the tables

Inside, the sorting crew is already moving. Canned goods on the left. Dry goods in the middle. Fresh food on the right, closest to the door, so it goes first.

We set it up like a small grocery store. Neighbors walk through and pick what they want. No one hands you a box of things you didn't choose. That matters to us. It's your food. You should get to pick it.

Our volunteer roster lists 85 active volunteers this year. On a typical Saturday, maybe a dozen of them are here. Everyone has a job, but nobody is stuck in one spot. If the line backs up, people move to where they're needed.

## 9:00 a.m. Doors open

This is my favorite part.

The first families are usually waiting outside. Some of them have been coming for years. I know their kids' names. I know who likes the spicy salsa and who will take every bag of rice we have.

Others are here for the first time. You can tell. They hold their sign-in form a little tighter. They look around before they step in. We try to make that first visit easy. Someone walks them through the tables. Someone tells them it's okay to take what they need.

This year, 310 unique families came through our doors, from our intake log. Together they made 2,080 grocery pickups, from our Saturday sign-in sheets. Behind every one of those numbers is a person who decided to walk in. That takes courage. We don't forget it.

## 10:30 a.m. The rush

Around mid-morning it gets loud. Kids are running between tables. Someone is asking if we have more eggs. Someone else found a recipe card in the bread section and wants to know if we have the ingredients.

We weigh everything that comes in and out. It's not glamorous, but it helps us plan. Our warehouse scale records show we handed out 96,000 pounds of food this year. That's a lot of Saturdays and a lot of very tired arms.

This is also when the swapping starts. One neighbor brings extra zucchini from home. Another trades a recipe for her mother's soup. The pantry stops feeling like a pantry and starts feeling like a front porch.

## 12:00 p.m. Closing up

By noon the tables are mostly empty. That's the goal. Food sitting on a shelf doesn't help anyone.

The cleanup crew breaks down the tables, sweeps the floor, and logs what's left. Then a few of us sit outside for a while. Nobody plans it. It just happens. We talk about who we saw, who we missed, and what we need more of next week.

Our volunteer shift log shows 4,200 hours this year. I think about that number on Saturdays at noon. Every one of those hours was somebody choosing to be here.

## The part you don't see

Saturdays are what people picture. But the work doesn't stop there.

On Thursdays, volunteers pack weekend snack bags for kids. Our school partner list has 75 kids on it. Over the school year, volunteers packed 3,600 bags, from our packing tally sheet. Teachers slip them into backpacks on Friday so no kid has to ask.

And all of it runs on support from neighbors. This year we raised $142,000, from our year-end bookkeeping. Most of that came in small monthly gifts. That steady support is what keeps the lights on and the shelves full.

## Come see for yourself

If you've never been to a Saturday, come. You don't need to sign up. Just show up and someone will find you a job.

And if you can't make it in person, you can still help keep the tables full.

Volunteer. Donate. Either one makes the next Saturday possible.

See you out back with the tomatoes.
