# SCRIPT — fix-it-saturday

**Voice:** set by the brand pack (`tts.kokoro.voice`)
**Voice direction:** Plain and warm, with forward momentum. Punctuation is load-bearing: questions rise, full stops land. Don't "clean it up."

Source: `source.md`. Every number below is from it.

---

## Line 1 — Hook (Frame 1)

    What happens to the lamp that flickers? The jacket with the broken zipper? The bike chain that slips? The chair with the wobbly leg?

## Line 2 — Problem (Frame 2)

    In most homes they sit in a closet for a while. Nobody knows how to fix them. Then they go in the trash.

## Line 3 — Claim (Frame 3)

    Alder Bay tried something else. On September twelfth the public library held its first Fix-It Saturday. You bring the broken thing. A neighbor helps you fix it. It costs nothing.

## Line 4 — Check in (Frame 4)

    Here is how it works. You check in at the front desk and say what is wrong. Then you wait at the right table.

## Line 5 — The tables (Frame 5)

    There are four tables. Each one handles one kind of repair. Bikes. Lamps and small electronics. Clothing. Furniture.

## Line 6 — Fix it together (Frame 6)

    A volunteer looks at it with you. You hold the tools. They explain each step. If a part is missing you leave with a list of what to buy. Nobody fixes it for you. You fix it together.

## Line 7 — Proof (Frame 7)

    The library's sign-in sheet tells the story. Sixty-four people came. They brought ninety-one items. The volunteer roster lists fourteen fixers. By closing time fifty-eight items worked again.

## Line 8 — Close (Frame 8)

    That is fifty-eight things saved from the trash. And sixty-four neighbors who know their stuff a little better. The next Fix-It Saturday is the second Saturday in November. Bring something broken.
